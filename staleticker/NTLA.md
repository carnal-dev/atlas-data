> The leader in *in-vivo* CRISPR — editing genes inside the body with a single infusion, rather than extracting cells. Two late-stage programs (ATTR amyloidosis and hereditary angioedema) with standout data.

### Bull case
- **In-vivo CRISPR is a genuine scientific moat** — proprietary editing + lipid-nanoparticle delivery, years ahead of most rivals. A one-time treatment vs. lifelong therapy.
- **nex-Z (ATTR):** FDA cleared Phase 3 trials back on track; ATTR is a ~$17B market currently served by chronic drugs.
- **HAE program:** ~87% reduction in attacks vs placebo — potentially a functional cure. Cash into 2028 funds the pivotal readouts.

### Bear case
- **Binary, pre-revenue clinical biotech** — value swings on trial outcomes and FDA decisions; prior clinical holds show the regulatory fragility.
- Gene editing carries long-tail safety scrutiny (off-target/immunogenicity).
- Will likely need more capital before any approval; dilution is a recurring feature.

**Thesis invalidation:** a Phase 3 failure or a safety signal in nex-Z or the HAE program, or an FDA hold that isn't quickly resolved.

### Path to multiples
- **10x:** nex-Z and HAE both reach approval and commercialize — Intellia becomes the first scaled in-vivo gene-editing franchise.
- **100x:** the platform generalizes across many genetic diseases (a "programmable cures" company) — enormous TAM, but many binary gates between here and there.

**Subjective 5–10yr odds:** P(≥3x) ~38% · P(≥10x) ~14% · P(major permanent loss) ~30%.
