> A leader in polymer **3D printing** (additive manufacturing) — the FDM and PolyJet systems used in prototyping, tooling, dental, and aerospace. The "value" name in a theme that has badly disappointed investors.

### Bull case
- **Cheap and de-risked on the balance sheet** — trades near ~1.2× sales with **$238M cash and no debt**; downside is partly cushioned.
- Real ~$570M revenue, an installed base, and a brand in a technology that still has long-run industrial promise (on-demand, distributed manufacturing).
- Cost cuts and a leaner structure could turn modest growth into operating leverage.

### Bear case
- **Additive manufacturing has been a serial value-destroyer** — repeated hype cycles, flat-to-declining revenue, and weak economics across the sector.
- **Tariff and legal costs** are pressuring an already loss-making P&L.
- No clear catalyst to re-accelerate growth; it's a mature, contested market.

**Thesis invalidation:** revenue keeps drifting flat/down and losses persist — confirming AM remains a structurally low-return industry rather than an inflection story.

### Path to multiples
- **A low-variance re-rating, not a moonshot** — 2–3x if cost cuts + a demand uptick restore profitability and the multiple normalizes; P(≥10x) ~5%.

**Subjective 5–10yr odds:** P(≥3x) ~28% · P(≥10x) ~5% · P(major loss) ~20%.

### Why it scores B
A net-cash balance sheet and cheap valuation (Valuation **60**) lift the score, but a mature, flat market (Reality Check **58**, Execution **45**) caps it at low-B. Fills the additive-manufacturing theme as the sane, balance-sheet-protected representative rather than a distressed microcap.

**Catalysts:** cost-out / margin recovery · defense-aero & dental demand · any AM-sector consolidation.
