> A niche "picks-and-shovels" semiconductor play: wafer-level test and burn-in systems. Originally a silicon-carbide story, now pivoting hard into AI-processor and datacenter burn-in.

### Bull case
- **Specialized IP** in wafer-level burn-in (FOX systems) — a real, defensible niche in semi test where few competitors operate.
- **AI/datacenter pivot** has reinvigorated the order book; management reinstated guidance citing AI-processor and datacenter visibility.
- Profitable, clean balance sheet, no meaningful debt — rare for a microcap.

### Bear case
- **Customer concentration** — historically dependent on one or two large customers; the SiC slowdown hit revenue hard before the AI pivot.
- **Lumpy orders** make quarters volatile; FY26 revenue still only ~$45–50M.
- **Valuation is rich** (~65x sales) — a lot of the AI ramp is already priced.

**Thesis invalidation:** the AI-burn-in design wins fail to convert to repeat orders, or a key customer in-sources test, returning Aehr to lumpy SiC-era revenue.

### Path to multiples
- **10x:** wafer-level burn-in becomes a standard step in AI-accelerator production and Aehr captures it broadly with diversified customers.
- **100x:** not realistic for a niche test-equipment maker without becoming a much broader semicap franchise.

**Subjective 5–10yr odds:** P(≥3x) ~30% · P(≥10x) ~7% · P(major permanent loss) ~18%.

### Why it scores B
Reality Check **65** (real revenue, profitable, real customers — though lumpy) and Replacement Risk **68**. Held back by small TAM, customer concentration, and a rich valuation (Valuation input 30). Solid semis exposure without the binary risk of pre-revenue names.
